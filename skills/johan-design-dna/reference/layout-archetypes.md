# Layout archetypes

Pick the archetype in the intake. These are starting structures, not templates: drop any section
that has no real content, and add sections the content needs. Each section gets one job.

## 1. Local service business (example: Lendav Hollandlane, roof and facade washing)

Visitor question: "Can they do my job, is it safe, what does it cost, how do I ask?"

1. Hero: real looped video of the work behind the headline, trust chips (max 3), price-from line,
   CTA + phone number.
2. The two or three services, as large clickable choices ("Kies waarvoor je kwam").
3. What it means for you: 3–4 concrete benefits in plain language.
4. Services overview with price-from per service.
5. Published prices (transparency is a selling point).
6. Before/after: only real photos. If none exist yet, say so honestly instead of using stock.
7. Comparison with the traditional method (a real table, if the comparison is real).
8. Short FAQ (3 questions, link to full FAQ).
9. Final CTA + contact block + company details footer.

## 2. Consultancy / expert service (example: Drone Approval Support)

Visitor question: "Do they understand my problem, can I trust them, what happens next?"

1. Hero: the visitor's problem as a question or statement, one-sentence promise, primary CTA
   (a free check or call), secondary "how it works".
2. The problem, made concrete (why this is hard without help).
3. How it works: a real sequence (numbered only because it is a sequence).
4. Proof of expertise: credentials, regulations handled, cases.
5. Who it is for / not for.
6. CTA with low threshold.

## 3. SaaS product (example: Complai)

Visitor question: "What does it do for me, show me, what does it cost?"

1. Hero: headline about the outcome, short sentence, CTA, then **the product demonstrated**:
   a choreographed scroll scene or infographic that shows the transformation the product makes.
2. Why now / what is at stake (one strong section, not four pain cards).
3. Product in use: 2–3 feature stories, each with its own visual that shows the feature working.
4. Pricing (only the tiers that exist; highlight one).
5. FAQ, final CTA.

## 4. Exuberant consumer / leisure (example: zoo, festival, kids)

Visitor question: "Is this fun, what can I do there, when and how much?"
Energy level 3 is likely; propose a display typeface (ask first).

1. Immersive hero: video, 3D or illustration that moves, big playful headline, ticket CTA.
2. Highlights as a scroll story (animals, acts, attractions), large imagery, motion.
3. Practical: opening hours, prices, getting there, in one clear block.
4. Social proof / moments.
5. Ticket CTA repeated, newsletter.

## Shared structural rules

- Content width `--wrap` (72rem); prose at `--measure`.
- Section spacing `--section-y`; generous whitespace is part of the calm look.
- Centered hero by default; inside sections, left-aligned text is fine and often better for reading.
- Use numbering only for real sequences. Use cards only when items are truly parallel and
  clickable; do not chop every section into identical rounded cards.
- One CTA style per page section; the primary CTA keeps the same label across the page.
